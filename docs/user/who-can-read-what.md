# Who can read what

Every fact answers three questions separately, and that is what lets several
people share one memory without everybody seeing everything.

**About** — who or what the fact belongs to. A person (`user:alice`), a group
(`group:famiglia`), or `global`. This is the axis that decides where the fact
is filed and who may govern it.

**Said by** — who reported it. Never the same field as *About*: *"Alice told us
Bob changed jobs"* is about Bob and said by Alice, and both are kept.

**Also readable by** — everybody else who may read it, on top of whoever it is
about. A person, a group, or several, listed on the fact.

**You read a fact when any one of the three names you.** You are who it is
about; or you are the one who said it; or you are named in *Also readable by* —
directly, or through a group you belong to, or because it is readable by
`global`, which everybody matches. Everything else is simply not there for you:
no error, no *access denied*, and on a page the sentence is replaced by
`[redacted]`.

## Changing it

On the [Facts](your-facts.md) page, **edit** a fact **about you** and use
**Change who may read it**. It applies straight away, and the receipt says what
changed. To put it back, make the opposite change.

You can also say it in ordinary conversation with your assistant — *"that
one's private"* — and in [the chat](the-chat.md).

## Groups

A group is a named set of people, created by your admin. Making a fact readable
by `group:famiglia` makes it readable by everyone in that group, and it stops
being readable by somebody taken out of it.

`global` is the one group nobody creates: it holds everyone, and it is meant
for general, public knowledge that belongs to no single person. Putting `global`
in a fact's *Also readable by* is how a personal fact is made public — which is
a different act from making it *about* `global`, and the Groups page says so.

## Guests

An assistant can be allowed to answer people it cannot identify — an
unrecognised voice, an unknown chat account. Those turns are **guest** turns:
they recall only public memory and store nothing at all. Nothing a guest says
becomes a fact, and nothing private is ever read out to one.

## Your standing rules

Separately from any single fact, you have a page of rules — reached from
[home](your-home.md) as *Your standing rules*, and first written in the
[welcome wizard](first-sign-in.md). *Keep anything about my health private.*
*Never store my address.* They are instructions to the memory rather than
things it remembers about you, and they apply to everything. That difference is
why forgetting a person removes their rules along with what they said: handed
to somebody else, a rule about you would become an instruction about you.
